Measure Intelligence by Speed

A metric to track the exponential growth of AI





Haifeng Jin

The Problem with AI Benchmarks

  • Traditional benchmarks focus on quality and accuracy.
  • They largely overlook processing speed.
  • However, many real-world applications are time-sensitive, like autonomous driving or customer service.

Existing Speed Metrics Aren't Enough

Metrics like Time to First Token (TTFT) and Tokens Per Second (TPS) have major limitations:

  • Token-centric: They measure token output, not the usefulness of the information.
  • Not multi-modal: They are ill-suited for a future with images, audio, and video.

A New Metric: Intelligence Bandwidth

Definition: The amount of useful information an AI can produce per unit of time.


$$ B = \frac{I}{t} $$

Where $B$ is intelligence bandwidth, $I$ is useful information, and $t$ is time.

How to Measure "Useful Information"?

This paper proposes three ways to approximate it:

  1. Benchmark Score / Time: How much useful work (score) is done per second.
  2. Information Theory: The information content (in bits) of the output per second.
  3. Raw Output Bits / Time: Simple, modality-agnostic, and used for the paper's experiments.

Impact on Human-AI Interaction

Just as network bandwidth enabled richer media on the internet (text → images → video), intelligence bandwidth will transform human-AI interaction.

Real-time interaction in a modality becomes feasible when AI generation speed surpasses human perception speed.

We're Already There for Text

  • Human reading speed: ~238 words per minute.
  • State-of-the-art AI speed: ~14,000 words per minute.

What's next?

Experimental Data: A Clear Trend

Intelligence Bandwidth (KB/s) of major AI models over time.

Observation: Exponential Growth!

The experimental data reveals a distinct exponential growth trend, particularly for image generators.

This observation allows us to formulate a predictive law, similar to Moore's Law for transistors.

Introducing: Jin's Law

The intelligence bandwidth of the best publicly available AI model doubles approximately every year.


$$ B(t) = B_0 \cdot 2^{(t-t_0)/T} $$

Where the doubling period $T = 1$ year.

Predictions from Jin's Law

  1. Images in text conversations (Near-term): With image generation latency dropping below text response times, images will soon be integrated as illustrations in chat.
  2. Real-time video interaction (3 Years): An 8x speed increase would make real-time video generation feasible. At the current doubling rate, this is about 3 years away.

Conclusion & Key Takeaways

  • Intelligence Bandwidth is a new metric to measure AI processing speed.
  • Jin's Law predicts this metric doubles annually, forecasting future capabilities.
  • We need a paradigm shift: Evaluate dynamic, served AI systems, not just static models.
  • Main takeaway: Evaluate AI for a task using the same criteria as for a human—considering both quality and speed.
